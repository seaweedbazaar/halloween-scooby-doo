PRINT TEXTURE (most important for the look; match Image 1):
- Colors are printed with visible Ben-Day halftone dots, especially on skin, pink, yellow and blue areas. Not smooth solid fills. Halftone dots on skin keep the skin exactly as light or dark as in Image 3.
- Slight color misregistration: color fills shifted a little off the black linework, leaving small white gaps along edges and color spilling past outlines.
- Inside the panels: a slightly yellowed newsprint look with faint grain, slight ink bleed, and a soft, slightly blurry scanned-page feel. The yellowing never applies to anyone's skin.
- Limited flat 4-color print palette: hot pink, lemon yellow, sky blue, grass green, orange, purple, brown, black. No smooth gradients, no airbrushed or soft shading, no modern cel shading.
- Glowing ghosts, eyes and lights are fine, but keep them in the same flat print colors with halftone dots.
- It must look like a genuine old printed comic, not a modern digital illustration made to look retro.

LINEWORK:
- Hand-inked with a brush: thick-to-thin varying line weight, slightly uneven and wobbly, not uniform digital lines.
- Shadows done with scratchy black hatching and feathering lines, especially in hair, fur and clothing folds, not with shaded color tones.
- Only hair that is actually black or very dark is filled with solid black ink, with a few flat blue or white highlight shapes. Every character keeps their own hair color (blonde, red, brown, gray, etc.); never change anyone's hair color to black.
- Simple 1970s comic proportions like Image 1: realistic adult bodies about 7 heads tall with SMALL heads, simple inked faces, small eyes with NO shine highlights, expressions shown through eyebrows and body language. NOT a Bitmoji, NOT an avatar, NOT a modern webcomic, NOT a caricature app. No big round eyes, no glossy highlights, no smooth vector lines.

LETTERING (must look hand-lettered in a real 1970s comic book; copy Image 2 exactly):
- Hand-lettered with a pen by a comic letterer, not a computer font. Every letter is drawn by hand, so repeated letters are never identical: slight differences in size, slant and stroke thickness.
- SMALL, DENSE TEXT like Image 2: ALL CAPS letters that are small relative to the panel (roughly the height of a character's eye), narrow and condensed (each letter about half as wide as it is tall), packed tightly together with almost no space between letters, normal single spaces between words, and tight line spacing (lines nearly touching). The text forms a compact, dense block.
- The bubble hugs the text block snugly, with only a thin margin of white around it. Never a big bubble with large, airy, widely spaced letters.
- Pen strokes go thick and thin, with slightly rough, soft ink edges that spread a little into the newsprint.
- The crossbar "I" (with little bars at the top and bottom) is used ONLY for the word "I" and in "I'M" or "I'VE". All other I's are a plain single stroke.
- Emphasized words are bolder, heavier and slightly slanted.
- Ellipses are drawn as three well-spaced dots.
- Each line of text is centered in the bubble.
- Speech bubbles are hand-drawn ovals with thin, slightly wobbly black outlines, shaped snugly around the text, with a short, thin, pointed wedge tail.
- AVOID: digital comic fonts, Comic Sans, perfectly even letters, wide letter spacing, thick uniform bubble outlines, rounded-rectangle bubbles.

SOUND EFFECTS (NEVER in bubbles):
